I like fiddling with a layout in Canva or Affinity. Before that it was the Adobe suite, and I liked that too. What I don't like is doing the same fiddling for the fortieth time.

Much of what I make is repeatable. A carousel has a cover, some body slides, and a closing slide, and it looks the same every time with different words in it. A journaling Bible page is a passage, a highlight, and a margin. So I've been building small browser tools to take the repetition out. It's partly a vibe coding exercise: I describe what I want, an AI writes the code, and I look at the result and say what's wrong with it.

There are three so far.

The Journaling Bible Generator lays scripture down two thirds of a square, with handwritten notes in the margin beside it. It's built specifically for my Living the Scandalous Life Substack. It started as a one-off in Canva for a post on Luke 9, and the measurements in the tool come from my published pages rather than from guesswork. I've used it for real.

The Kintsugi Overlay Generator loads a black and white photo, lets me draw gold lines over it, and exports the result. It's for the same Substack, whose feature images are all black and white. The original idea was that the tool would find the gold in the photo's own shadows. When it came to actually making the images, I drew over them instead, because I knew what I wanted the gold to say and the shadows didn't. So that half is gone. What's left is a brush, an eraser, and a colour picker. I've used this one too.

The Carousel Maker is the big one, with the most templates and the most options. It's also the one I haven't posted anything from. I've built it and played with it, and I haven't yet exported a slide and put it in front of anyone. That's a different test from whether it works, and I'd rather say it hasn't had it than pretend otherwise.

The simpler two earned their place because I used them, and using them is how I found out what to cut. The kintsugi tool is smaller than I first built it for exactly that reason. I expect the carousel maker to lose some of its options the same way once a real carousel goes out.

All three are free on the tools page, and they run in your browser.
