Updated: May 2026

The tools I use for work and everything adjacent to it. Updated when something changes.

Hardware

My main machine is a 2021 M1 iMac, which still feels fast enough that I have no reason to replace it. I use a reMarkable 2 for reading, note-taking, and getting thoughts down without a screen full of notifications competing for attention. My phone is an iPhone SE (3rd generation), because I don't need my phone to be exciting.

Writing

I draft and develop almost everything in Obsidian, which serves as my second brain. Flat file structure, YAML front matter, wikilinks, and a level of organisational ambition that occasionally outpaces my organisational discipline. It's where course content, blog drafts, theology notes, and RPG session prep all live side by side, which says something about my working life.

For prose editing I use HemingwayApp to catch overwriting, and Writhm for voice and readability checking. Both are useful correctives when a draft has spent too long in my own head.

AI and Automation

Claude (Anthropic) is my primary AI tool, used for content drafting, research, structural thinking, and building out workflows. I use Cowork alongside it for desktop automation: content pipelines that take a draft from Google Drive through to LinkedIn, Substack, and Bluesky without manual reposting.

For local LLM work I run Ollama or LM Studio with Gemma 4, keeping processing on-device when privacy matters or when I want to experiment without sending everything through a cloud API.

Zapier connects the backend systems: ThriveCart purchases trigger Teachery enrolments, which trigger Mailchimp sequences, which trigger follow-up flows. It's more plumbing than glamour, but it works.

Course and Content Platform

The Holistic Email Academy runs on Teachery for course delivery, ThriveCart for checkout, and Mailchimp for email automation and student communications. All the sites (jonathanpay.com, holisticemailmarketing.com, holisticemailacademy.com) run on WordPress with Yoast SEO.

Video

I edit video in Descript. It's good for the kind of work I do (course content, talking heads, sting production) without the overhead of a full editing suite. The transcript-first editing model suits how I think about content anyway.

The Bridge Layer

Google Drive sits underneath most of this as the connective tissue between tools. Cowork reads from and writes to Drive, Zapier pulls from it, and it serves as the staging ground for content before it goes anywhere public. It's not glamorous, but it's the thing that makes the automation pipeline possible.
