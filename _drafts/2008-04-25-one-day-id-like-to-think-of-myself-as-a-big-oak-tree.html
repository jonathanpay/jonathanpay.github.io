Maturity takes time. Painfully obvious but I seem to have overlooked such wisdom.

On my churches men's weekend (shotguns, quad bikes, a bucking bronco, bonfires and a hog roast…) I was prayed for, after answering a ministry call for maturity during one of our worship times. While being prayed for I was given a few words on the subject of maturity, a subject I want to know more about and experience more of (as I feel like a 7 year old in a 20 year olds body)

Whenever someone brings a word for me it usually involves my height or my general size, I am a pretty big guy after all. He just reminded me that an Oak tree has to grow to the size it becomes. My Christian faith (as well as my personal maturity), I feel, is at the maturity level of a young tree, I have a few rings and branches but not much fruit growing yet.

My goal is to be bigger in faith and maturity and it has not happened even to the level I'd expected myself to be at by the time I was to hit 21, let alone in the last year. The worst part is that I've picked up on it (I don’t have as many pennies to my name as I thought I would, I have neither a drivers licence or car, I still play video games that I played in 7th grade.)

As a matter of fact When this subject was brought up at the men's weekend, I'd become frustrated with myself at my lack of progress on any level; spiritual, financial… Certainly I've seen a bit of growth in the last year, and the bible says to be child like in faith, heck not so long ago I posted on child-like faith.

I’d said then that I still needed some maturing and its stands more now than ever. Maturity and frustration of the slow process was what was brought to me and seeing just how relevant it was to me I felt it held a lot of weight and have found it encouraging and helpful to my attempts to mature as a person and in my Christian faith.

One day I will look back and remember the point when became the Oak tree.
