This post stays pinned at the top. It's less a post than a statement of intent, and I'll come back and update it as the year goes on.

I turn 40 in 2027. I'm 39 now, which is old enough to have noticed a pattern in how people get known for things, and young enough to think I can still do something about it.

The pattern goes like this. Someone gets good at one thing, or at least visible for it. They build an audience around it. The audience expects more of the same. A few years later they want to talk about something else, and it gets called a pivot. A rebrand. Sometimes a betrayal. As if they'd been one thing all along and suddenly became another.

I don't think that's what happens. I think most people were several things the whole time, and only one of them was getting any attention.

What this site has been

If you've landed here, you've probably seen the professional side first. Email marketing. I've done it since before Gmail existed, learnt it in my mum's home office, and still do it alongside her now. That's the top half of the homepage. It's honest, and it pays the bills.

Then there's the writing. Most of what I've published here this year is about faith. What I believe, what I've had to unlearn, what scripture says when you stop using it as a rulebook. That's personal in a way that email strategy isn't. Some people who come for the subject lines will find it odd. A few will find it unwelcome.

There are Life and Fun categories too. They've got a handful of posts in them: a film that rewired my head, World Stroke Day, a few pieces on tabletop games. But they're the occasional visitors. Work and faith live here. Everything else drops by.

What's missing

The bio I use everywhere goes: Christian. Husband. Father. Feminist, increasingly socialist, always anti-fascist. Antipodean immigrant in the UK. Cinephile. Jack-of-all email marketing trades. Board game and RPG enthusiast. Stroke survivor.

That's nine things. The site mostly covers two.

The films I can't stop thinking about. The games I run for friends, and the ones gathering dust on the shelf. What it's like to raise two kids on the other side of the world from where I grew up. What a stroke does to how you think about time. None of that is a secret. I just haven't made room for it.

Paul has a passage in 1 Corinthians about the body having many parts. The eye can't say to the hand, "I don't need you." He's talking about the church, not about one person, and I don't want to stretch it further than it goes. But I keep coming back to it. A body that only shows off its hands is still a whole body. It's just hiding most of itself.

The bits that rub

Not all of these parts sit comfortably next to each other. I'd be lying if I said they did.

My faith shapes my politics, and my politics aren't what a lot of Christians would expect. Writing openly about either can cost you work in an industry where the safe move is to say nothing that isn't about open rates. I work with my mum, so my reputation and hers are tangled together. I don't get to pretend my choices only land on me. Some clients will read a piece on Christian nationalism and quietly decide I'm not for them. That's their call to make.

The tensions run the other way too. I read the Bible as something other than a rulebook, which puts me at odds with some of the people I grew up in church with. I'm a feminist inside a tradition that has spent a long time arguing about whether women should be allowed to preach. And I spend my working days helping brands sell things, while writing here about a Jesus who had sharp words for the rich.

I don't have a tidy answer for any of that. I'm not sure I want one yet. What I want is to stop editing myself down into a single, marketable shape and see what happens when I don't.

That doesn't mean everything goes up. Some parts of life belong to my family, and they stay there. Being more of myself in public doesn't mean being all of myself in public.

The thesis

So here's the project for the year between now and 40.

Write about more of it. Not equally, and not to a schedule, but enough that this site reads like a person with a job, a faith, a family, and a shelf full of board games.

In practice, that looks like:

  • Giving Life and Fun regular posts, not occasional ones. Films, games, the stroke, being Australian in England.
  • Keeping the faith writing going, and no longer treating it as the awkward bit at the bottom of the page.
  • Doing good work, and letting it sit next to everything else without apologising for either.
  • Writing about the places where these things rub against each other, instead of keeping them in separate rooms.

I'll come back to this post as the year goes on and add what's worked, what hasn't, and what I've changed my mind about. If I've learnt anything from 39 years, it's that I'll change my mind about something.

If you came here for email, the email's still here. If you came for the faith writing, so is that. If you stick around for the rest, I'd like that.
