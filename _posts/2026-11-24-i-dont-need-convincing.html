I've booked the tickets. Friday 18 December, I'm taking the day off to watch Avengers: Doomsday and Dune: Part Three back to back. I'll probably enjoy both. I'm not as hardcore a fan as some people, but I'm more of one than others, and either way the decision was made a long time ago.

It's been a while since I've done a day like this. I've done a couple of overnight marathons at The Prince Charles Cinema in Leicester Square, including the Avengers Phase Two one, but the last proper multi-film day I can remember was on my own, on 3 February 2014. I caught a bus from Canterbury to the Carlton in Westgate-on-Sea, a lovely little cinema where tickets were about £2.50, and watched three films: 12 Years a Slave, Jack Ryan: Shadow Recruit, and The Wolf of Wall Street. I timed it all perfectly, except that I missed the last bus home. The taxi cost about as much as three tickets at a regular cinema would have, so I didn't save any money that day. It was still a real treat, because I love going to the cinema. I love going to the movies.

Before the pandemic I had a Cineworld Unlimited card. As long as I saw two films a month it paid for itself, and it was part of my self-care. I haven't made the time to do that again in this new stage of my life. I don't think I can afford it right now, and I have the responsibilities of my daughters to balance as well.

If I know I'm going to see a film, I don't need to be convinced, and I don't need to be hyped up. Those trailers aren't for me, I've seen bits of each.

The prime example, more than any Marvel film, was the Star Wars sequels. When The Force Awakens was coming out, I think I saw a teaser, and then I decided I wasn't going to watch any more trailers. When I went to the cinema to see something else, I literally blocked my ears and closed my eyes, and if I was with someone, I asked them to tell me when it was over. It was only ever thirty seconds to a minute. I wanted no idea of what was coming. I wanted to go into those films as unexpected as possible, and it's something I've tried to follow through with generally when it comes to going to the cinema.

Some days it feels like I'm screaming at people not to watch things they already know they're going to watch, and already know they're going to get excited about. There's so much hype, so much content, and so many people dissecting all of it in videos. That's only increased since Infinity War and Endgame (and it was building before them). At what point do people acknowledge the diminishing returns of being right about something they worked out from a trailer?

The final Doomsday trailer is out now, and I saw a response from someone who has become a bit of an influencer on the Marvel scene. She's one of many, I'd guess. She was upset because a line in the trailer would have been an incredible moment in the film, if she'd had no foreknowledge of it. I agree with her. I don't think things like that should be in trailers. Will it change how she engages with this stuff? Probably not. She makes money presenting these things, doing theories as she watches, doing reactions and responses. That's on her, and it's unfortunate.

I still think it's premature, a bit foolish even, for studios to put certain moments in trailers, knowing full well that people will watch and dissect them to work out what happens before it happens. Not everyone goes to the movies the way I do. I recognise that. But plenty of people are exposing themselves to the very thing that will spoil the cinema experience for them, and I think more of them need to be aware of it.

Then again, it doesn't all run one way.

There seems to be a resurgence of smaller studios doing more interesting things, and there a trailer actually helps, and matters. Some films I know about because I've been anticipating them along with everyone else, or they're part of a franchise I'm following, or they come from a director, producer, or cast I'm familiar with. Those need less convincing. Then there are new things. Neon has Artificial coming out, Luca Guadagnino's film with Andrew Garfield as Sam Altman, about the few days in 2023 when he was fired from OpenAI and then reinstated. I watched the trailer. I was probably already going to try and see it, but now I really want to.

(Yes, Guadagnino and Garfield are exactly the kind of names that should have sold me already. The trailer still moved me from probably to definitely.)

So it comes at me from both sides. The rule has soft edges. Most things do, I've come to accept. There are fewer black and white extremes than I used to think, and a lot more grey in the middle. Fuzzy borders.

But I've made a decision about how I operate. If I'm already going, I skip the trailer. If I'm not sure, I let the trailer do its job. That's how I go to the movies. Consider trying it.

One complication: Doomsday now opens in the UK on Wednesday 16 December, two days before my double bill. So that's two days of keeping my head down online. We'll see how I get on.
